# Go-to-market (founder mode)

## The reframe

You are not selling a browser extension. You are selling **task completion for
vulnerable populations**. The people who use it (veterans, benefits applicants) never pay.
The people who pay are the organizations whose funding and mission depend on those people
*finishing* things — and who today have no instrumentation on why people abandon.

## Buyers, ranked by how fast they'll move

1. **VSOs / veteran nonprofits.** Mission-aligned, fast to pilot, emotionally bought-in.
   Sell the dashboard that proves fewer vets abandon claims. Best beachhead.
2. **County / city social services.** Bigger checks, slower cycle. Sell co-branded
   deployment inside their existing benefits portal + a caseload completion view.
3. **Health systems & legal aid.** Same pattern, different forms (intake, appeals).
4. **Benefits / fintech / health platforms.** License the Task Graph API to embed your
   "next clear step" engine in their product. Highest leverage, latest stage.

## Pricing (illustrative)

- **Individuals:** free forever.
- **Nonprofit / VSO dashboard:** ~$300–800/mo per org (analytics + caseload view).
- **County / agency co-branded deployment:** ~$20k–50k/yr (white-label + analytics + SLA).
- **Task Graph API:** usage-based per resolved task, volume tiers.

## The wedge → land → expand motion

1. **Wedge:** ship the free extension that works on *any* form. Distribution + trust.
2. **Land:** one VSO or one county pilot. Instrument completion before/after.
3. **Prove:** "abandonment on the disability claim flow dropped X%." That single chart
   is the entire sales deck.
4. **Expand:** from one flow to all flows in that agency, then to neighboring agencies
   (they talk to each other and share vendors).

## Why this defends itself

- **Data network effect:** every completed task improves the task-graph templates, which
  improves completion, which produces more data. Competitors start from zero curation.
- **Trust moat:** the privacy stance ("no content ever leaves the device") is a feature
  government buyers specifically need and most AI vendors can't honestly claim.
- **Mission moat:** "no veteran pays for the bridge back to help" is both right and a
  procurement advantage with public-sector buyers.

## First 90 days

- Week 1–4: extension works on 3 unseen real forms (VA, SNAP, a housing portal).
- Week 5–8: backend analytics + a one-screen partner dashboard.
- Week 9–12: one signed VSO pilot, baseline completion measured, first before/after chart.

## Risks to name honestly

- Nano availability varies by device; always ship template-text fallback.
- Government sites change DOM often; the task-graph + analytics catch breakage early.
- Procurement is slow; the free individual tier keeps growth alive while deals close.
